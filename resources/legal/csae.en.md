# Child Safety Standards — Akukas

**Last Updated: September 4, 2026**

Akukas has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE). This page sets out
our standards, how we enforce them, and how anyone can report a concern. It applies to every
part of the service: public posts, comments, profiles, groups, hashtags and direct messages.

## 1. What is prohibited

The following are strictly forbidden on Akukas and are grounds for immediate, permanent
account termination:

- **1.1.** Child sexual abuse material (CSAM) — any image, screenshot, video or text that
  depicts, describes or promotes the sexual abuse or sexual exploitation of a minor.
- **1.2.** Sexualising a minor in any way, including edited, drawn, animated or
  computer-generated depictions.
- **1.3.** Grooming — any attempt to build a relationship with a minor for sexual purposes,
  including soliciting images, contact details or offline meetings.
- **1.4.** Sextortion — threatening to publish a minor's intimate images to obtain money,
  further images, or any other benefit.
- **1.5.** Trafficking, advertising, soliciting, selling or seeking access to a minor for
  sexual purposes.
- **1.6.** Sharing, linking to, or directing users toward CSAE material hosted anywhere else.
- **1.7.** Using the service to identify, contact or locate a minor for any of the above.

These rules apply regardless of intent, framing, humour, or whether the material is claimed to
be fictional.

## 2. Age requirement

Akukas is not intended for children. Users must be at least 13 years old, and users under 18
must have the consent of a parent or legal guardian, as set out in our Terms and Conditions.
Accounts we identify as belonging to a child under our minimum age are removed.

## 3. How to report

**In the app:** every post, comment, user profile and conversation has a **Report** option in
its menu. Choose the reason that fits and add any detail in the free-text field. Reports go
directly to our moderation queue for human review.

**By email:** you can report CSAE concerns directly to **privacy@akukas.com**, including a link
or username where possible. Reports may be made anonymously.

**Please do not download, save, or re-share suspected CSAM in order to report it.** A link,
username or description is enough for us to act, and reproducing the material is itself
unlawful in most jurisdictions.

## 4. How we respond

- **4.1.** Reports involving a minor's safety are prioritised ahead of all other moderation
  work and are reviewed by a human moderator.
- **4.2.** Confirmed CSAE content is removed from the service.
- **4.3.** The account responsible is permanently terminated, and we take reasonable steps to
  prevent the same person from re-registering.
- **4.4.** We preserve the relevant account data and content as required for reporting to, and
  cooperation with, the competent authorities.
- **4.5.** We report apparent child sexual abuse material to the relevant law enforcement
  authorities and, where applicable, to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children
  (NCMEC), and we comply with lawful requests for information.
- **4.6.** Enforcement decisions are recorded in an internal audit log with the reason and the
  moderator who took the action.

## 5. What we do not do

We are explicit about our limits so that our standards are not overstated:

- We do **not** routinely scan or inspect the contents of private cloud storage or direct
  messages. Human review of private content occurs only when it is explicitly reported by a
  participant, or when we are required to act by law.
- We do not currently operate automated CSAM hash-matching. Detection today depends on user
  reports and human moderator review, which is why reporting matters and why we act on every
  report we receive.

## 6. Appeals

If you believe your account was actioned in error, you may contact **privacy@akukas.com** for
review. We do not reinstate accounts terminated for confirmed CSAE violations.

## 7. Contact

Child safety reports and enquiries: **privacy@akukas.com**

If a child is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first. Reporting to
us is not a substitute for contacting law enforcement.
